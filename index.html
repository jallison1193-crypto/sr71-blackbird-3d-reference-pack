3. Lockheed Coordinate System (FS, BL, WL) for Sub-Assembly Placement

Lockheed's engineering datum defines coordinates in inches:

  • Fuselage Station (FS): Longitudinal distance aft from a virtual datum ahead of the nose (FS 200.0 ≈ nose radome tip on standard SR-71A station charts; aft tailcone / drag-chute fairing terminates near FS 1489.0, spanning 1,289 in = 107 ft 5 in).
  • Butt Line (BL): Lateral distance left/right from fuselage centerline (BL 0.0).
  • Water Line (WL): Vertical height above horizontal reference plane below the belly (WL 100.0 ≈ fuselage chine/reference horizontal plane).

4. Key Airframe Surface & Silhouette Nuances (Critical for Accuracy)

  • Blended Chine Cross-Section ("Cobra Hood"):
  • The forward fuselage is not a simple cylinder: lateral sharp chines blend smoothly from the nose tip all the way into the 60° delta wing leading edge, generating vortex lift at supersonic speeds and reducing radar cross-section.
  • Inward Engine Nacelle Toe-In & Downward Droop:
  • The J58 inlet nacelles are toed inward slightly toward the fuselage centerline (~1.2°–1.5° toe-in) and drooped downward (~2.5°–3° nose-down incidence) at the front so the translating inlet spikes align directly with the local Mach 3.2 under-chine shockwave/upwash flowfield! Modeling them strictly parallel to Y and Z is the #1 mistake on amateur SR-71 models.
  • Conical Camber on Outer Wing Leading Edges:
  • Outboard of the engine nacelles, the delta wing leading edges droop downward in a conical camber curve, and the wingtips curve slightly downward.
  • Chordwise Skin Corrugations:
  • Both upper and lower wing surfaces between the fuselage and nacelles (and outboard) feature prominent chordwise stamped corrugations (expansion beads) so the Ti-13V-11Cr-3Al skin can expand thermally at 500–600 °F without buckling the spars. Model these via high-resolution normal/displacement maps or array-instanced bevel strips.
  • Composite "Pie-Slice" Sawtooth Chines:
  • Along the chine edges and wing trailing edges, triangular ("dog-tooth" / sawtooth) radar-absorbing phenyl-silane composite tiles alternate with titanium structure.

5. Cockpit Modeling & Color/Material Guide for Blender

  • Interior Structural Color: Dark neutral gray (FS 36231 Dark Gull Gray) tub bulkheads and canopy frames, contrasted against matte black (FS 37038) instrument bezels and sub-panels.
  • Ejection Seats (Lockheed C-2 / Weber SR-1 Zero-Zero): Gloss/semi-gloss black structural frame, bright orange-red cushions and headrest, silver/steel harness hardware, yellow-and-black striped D-ring ejection pull handle between the knees, and zinc-chromate gold hydraulic canopy actuator cylinder behind the seat.
  • Signature SR-71 Instruments to Feature in Close-Up Camera Moves:
  • Triple Display Indicator (TDI): Digital drum counter + analog strip top-left of center panel showing KEAS, Mach (past 3.2+), and Altitude (past 85,000 ft).
  • Inlet Spike & Forward Bypass Position Indicators: Dual-needle gauges monitoring left/right spike translation (26 in travel) and shockwave duct pressure ratio (unstart prevention).
  • Periscope / Optical Viewfinder: Center-top of Pilot's glare shield (gives rearward/horizon view) and center of RSO's panel (optical navigation/sensor tracking).
  • Master Caution Matrix: Large illuminated amber/red grid on the right forward panel (infamous for requiring two hands to press reset).

2. Pratt & Whitney J58 (JT11D-20) Bare Engine Hardware Reference Photos (360° Plumbing & Bypass Tubes)

Purpose: In Phase 2 of your pipeline, examining or clicking parts of the exploded 3D SR-71A model triggers contextual engineering callouts and background history. Hotspot Callout Cards (12 Core Subsystems)

1. Airframe Titanium Skin & Chordwise Corrugations (06_Inner_And_Outer_Wings)

  • Headline: 85% Beta-Titanium Airframe Built to Grow in Flight
  • Operating Temp: 450 °F – 600 °F (232 °C – 316 °C) average skin; 950 °F+ (510 °C+) at leading edges & exhaust
  • Snippet: At sustained Mach 3.2 cruise, aerodynamic friction heats the airframe beyond the structural softening point of aluminum. Lockheed Skunk Works fabricated 85% of the structure from titanium alloys (Ti-13V-11Cr-3Al). Because the aircraft expands several inches in flight, wing skins are stamped with chordwise corrugations acting as thermal expansion joints—causing fuel panels to seep JP-7 on the ground until supersonic heat seals the airframe tight.

2. Translating Axisymmetric Inlet Spike (Translating_Inlet_Spike)

  • Headline: Computer-Controlled Shockwave Trap (54% of Total Thrust!)
  • Travel Distance: 26 inches (0.66 m) axial translation (Mach 1.6 → Mach 3.2+)
  • Snippet: Above Mach 2.5, the inlet nacelles generate more propulsive thrust than the rotating turbojet core itself (~54% inlet pressure recovery vs. ~17.6% turbojet core and ~28.4% afterburner/ejector). Starting at Mach 1.6, the conical spike retracts aft 1.625 inches per 0.1 Mach (up to 26 inches), capturing the supersonic shockwave inside the throat and decelerating air from Mach 3.2 to subsonic Mach 0.4 inches ahead of the compressor face.

3. Pratt & Whitney J58 (JT11D-20) Continuous Bleed-Bypass Turboramjet (PW_J58_Core_And_6_Bypass)

  • Headline: Hybrid Turbojet-Assisted Ramjet (32,500 lbf Thrust Each)
  • Signature Feature: 6 External 4th-Stage Bleed-Bypass Ducts + TEB Chemical Ignition
  • Snippet: At Mach 3+, inlet compression heats incoming air to 800 °F (427 °C) before entering the compressor. Pratt & Whitney solved this by opening bleed valves behind the 4th compressor stage, routing ~20% of compressed air through six large external pipes directly into the afterburner. Each engine carries a sealed tank of pyrophoric Triethylborane (TEB)—enough for 16 injections—which bursts into green flame on contact with air to ignite low-volatility JP-7 fuel.
